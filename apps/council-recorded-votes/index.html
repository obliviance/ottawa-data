Ottawa's recorded votes

Ontario doesn't require municipalities to publish how councillors vote. Of the 7,621 motions Ottawa council has handled since the 2022 election, only 334 got a recorded division — the rest carried on consent, with no record of who wanted what.

On those 334, this is how often each member landed on the losing side — their dissent rate. It's a rough map of where each councillor sits relative to the majority that forms around the mayor.

The compiled record comes from howtheyvoted.ca, which reads it out of the meeting minutes by hand, because the city publishes it nowhere as data.

The two ends of the room

G. Darouze  ↔  S. Menard 23% agreement over 124 shared votes
D. Brown  ↔  I. Skalski 95% agreement over 77 shared votes

Darouze (rural Osgoode) and Menard (downtown Capital) vote the same way barely once in four recorded divisions. Even so, across all 324 councillor pairs, 30% agree more than four times in five — when Ottawa council splits, it isn't sharply factional.

How this was made

Each recorded motion has a for count and an against count; the larger side is “the majority” and a councillor “dissents” when they're on the other one. Members with fewer than 20 recorded votes are dropped; pairs with fewer than 15 shared votes are left out of the agreement figures. Ties count as carrying.

Only about 4% of motions reach a recorded vote, so this describes the contested slice of council business, not all of it. Consent-agenda items — most of the work — leave no trace here.
