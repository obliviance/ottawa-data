Your ward, 2026

What the councillor you have actually did, and who is asking to replace them. A record, not a ranking — nothing here is scored, and the wards are in number order.

Ottawa elects a mayor, 24 councillors and trustees to four school boards. Nominations closed on 21 August, so every name below is final. Two seats are already decided — one candidate each.

For a sitting councillor, the strongest evidence available is not what they say now but how they voted and whether they showed up. That is what the first block of each card shows.

What isn't here

No platforms, policies or party affiliation. Ontario municipal elections are non-partisan by law, the nomination form is not a CV, and no public body collects candidate platforms — so for newcomers there is genuinely nothing to compare.

Rather than imply a comparison the data can't support, each candidate is listed with the website and social links they filed with the City Clerk. Nothing here was gathered from anywhere else.

How this was made

Candidates, wards and school boards come from the city's own election web service. The voting record and attendance come from howtheyvoted.ca, which compiles them by hand because the city publishes them nowhere as data. Service and tax figures come from Open Ottawa.

Only about 4% of motions reach a recorded vote — the rest carry on consent. Dissent rate describes that contested slice and nothing else, which is why the vote count sits beside every rate: 19% of 90 votes and 43% of 235 are not the same kind of number.

Attendance is from howtheyvoted rather than the city, because — it begins nearly two years into the term.

Ward 20 changed hands mid-term and both records are shown. Councillors appear under several different spellings across sources, so every figure is joined through an explicit, published alias table rather than by matching names.
